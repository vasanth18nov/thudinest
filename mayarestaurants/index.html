Our story

A mother’s recipes, laid out on a leaf.

Maya began as a way to keep a family’s cooking alive. Co-founder Ramesh Kumar opened it to share his late mother’s recipes, dishes like Mutton Chukka that you rarely find on a banana-leaf menu.

After Brickfields and Bangsar, Solaris Mont Kiara became our third kitchen in 2024, run with co-founder Maynicca Rajamanikam. The rules are the old ones. Vegetables go on the right of the leaf so they’re eaten first. Every meal carries something sweet, something sour and something with heat. And the rice and vegetables keep coming until you fold your leaf.

Signature experiences

Four reasons people cross the city for Maya.

01

வாழை இலை விருந்து · the banana-leaf feast

The Leaf

Ponni rice at the centre, two vegetables on the right, then fried chicken or fish, papadam, lime pickle, sour chilli and mango chutney, and six curries poured over, one after another. Rasam comes to cleanse. Refills come until you fold the leaf toward you.

Express sets from RM 11.90 · Mon–Fri from 11am

See the sets
02

Maya’s signature dish

Kari Kepala Ikan

Fish head curry started in a roaring wok with garlic, shallot, green chilli and a storm of chilli powder, loosened with tamarind, then finished slowly in claypot with okra and tomato. It comes out tangy, deep and brick-red, and it’s made for the middle of the table.

Serves the table · order ahead for groups of six and more

Chef’s specials
03

From Ramesh’s mother’s kitchen

Mutton Chukka

A dry, pepper-dark roast of tender mutton with tomato, whole chilli and curry leaf. It’s rare on banana-leaf menus, and it’s on ours so more people get to taste it. Put it beside your rice, or eat it straight from the bowl.

On the leaf or à la carte · lunch and dinner

Every morning from 7am

Morning Tiffin

Thosai crisping on the tawa, soft idli, flaky parotta and roti canai, with sambar, three chutneys and a pulled teh tarik. If you’re properly hungry, ask for the curry thosai with chicken or mutton.
