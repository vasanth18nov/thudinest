01 Our Story

A village oasis in the heart of the city

Long before Passage Thru’ India, our founder was a globetrotting marketing director for a leading fashion house. Travelling India end to end, he fell for its kaleidoscope of colours, its tapestry of traditions and a symphony of flavours that changes every few hundred kilometres.

In 1993 he brought that journey home to Kuala Lumpur. An artist as much as a restaurateur, he painted the murals across our walls and ceilings himself, so that stepping in from Jalan Bukit Bintang feels like arriving in a village far from the city’s hustle.

Three decades on, our kitchen still works the way he intended: masalas pounded by hand and made from scratch, breads slapped against the walls of a coal-fired tandoor, and biryanis sealed and finished slowly over firewood.

“A canvas where every flavour tells a story, and every dish paints a picture.”

— The founder’s story

03 Journey Through India

Six regions, one table

Choose a stop on the route to discover the kitchens that shaped our menu.

North · The Himalaya

Kashmir

The valley of saffron

In the high valleys, the wazwan feast is a ceremony of patience. Kashmiri chillies lend colour rather than fire, while fennel and dried ginger bring a slow, gentle warmth.

At our table
Roghan Josh · Kashmirian Lollipops
Spice notes
Kashmiri chilli, fennel, dried ginger

North-west · The five rivers

Punjab

Heartland of the tandoor

Here the clay oven is the heart of every village. Breads blister against its walls, and butter, cream and smoke meet in the dishes that carried Indian cooking around the world.

At our table
Murgh Tikka Butter Masala · Paneer Makhani · Punjabi Samosa · Naan
Spice notes
Garam masala, kasuri methi, ajwain

North · Lucknow

Awadh

Royal kitchens of the Nawabs

The cooks of Lucknow perfected dum — sealing a pot with dough and letting it finish in its own steam. That patience lives on in our firewood dum biryanis and the Royal Awadh Platter.

At our table
Firewood Dum Goat Ribs Biryani · Seekh Kebab · Royal Awadh Platter
Spice notes
Saffron, mace, green cardamom, rose

East · The delta

Bengal

Mustard, rivers & sweetness

Bengal cooks with mustard and the five-spice panch phoron, and never ends a meal without something sweet. Our Bengali Chicken Chaap is marinated in yoghurt and a Bengali spice blend, then pan-grilled.

At our table
Bengali Chicken Chaap · Fish Dopiaza · Rasmalai
Spice notes
Panch phoron, mustard, nigella

South-central · Hyderabad & Andhra

The Deccan

Fire of the plateau

The Deccan loves heat and tang: tamarind, green chilli and curry leaf. Andhra’s chepala pulusu — our Andhra Fish Curry — is fiery, sour and utterly addictive; Hyderabad gives us baghare baingan and the dum biryani.

At our table
Andhra Fish Curry · Firewood Dum Chicken Biryani · Baghare Baingan
Spice notes
Tamarind, Guntur chilli, curry leaf

Deep south · Tamil Nadu & Kerala

Chettinad & Malabar

The spice coast

Black pepper and star anise define the merchant kitchens of Chettinad, while Kerala’s coast brings coconut, curry leaves and the catch of the day. Journey’s end — and the start of a long, lazy lunch.

At our table
Chettinad Chicken Masala · Kerala Chemmeen Biryani · Madras Fried Fish
Spice notes
Black pepper, star anise, coconut, curry leaf
