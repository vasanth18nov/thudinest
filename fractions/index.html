Lesson 1 of 8

What a fraction means

Cut one thing into equal pieces. A fraction tells you how many of those pieces you have.

The bottom number (the denominator) says how many equal pieces one whole is cut into. The top number (the numerator) says how many of those pieces you have.

So 38 means: cut it into 8 equal pieces and take 3 of them.

Remember

Bottom: how many pieces make one whole.
Top: how many pieces you’ve got.

Careful

The pieces must be the same size. A cake cut into 4 uneven slices is not cut into quarters.

Tap the pieces, or use the + and − buttons.

Bigger bottom, smaller pieces

One piece of each. The more pieces you cut, the smaller each one gets, so 18 is less than 12.

Lesson 2 of 8

Same amount, new name

Cut every piece of 23 in half. Now you have 4 pieces out of 6. Nothing was added and nothing was eaten, so 23 and 46 are the same amount. They are called equivalent fractions.

Going the other way is called simplifying: divide the top and the bottom by a number that goes into both, and keep going until no number does.

Remember

Multiply or divide the top and the bottom by the same number. The amount stays the same.

Simplify it yourself

Pick a number that goes into both the top and the bottom. Keep going until there are none left.

Lesson 3 of 8

Which one is bigger?

When the bottoms match, the pieces are the same size, so the fraction with the bigger top wins.

When the bottoms are different, rename both fractions so they have the same bottom (lesson 2), then compare the tops.

Remember

Same bottom: bigger top is bigger.
Same top: smaller bottom is bigger, because its pieces are bigger.

Shortcut

Cross-multiply. For 35 and 47: 3 × 7 = 21 and 4 × 5 = 20. 21 is bigger, so 35 is bigger.

Lesson 4 of 8

Adding fractions

You can only add pieces of the same size. One half plus one third isn’t “two” of anything, until you cut both into sixths: 3 sixths and 2 sixths make 5 sixths.

Pick a sum, then press Next step.

Remember

Make the bottoms the same. Add the tops. Keep the bottom. Simplify.

Careful

Never add the bottoms. 12 + 12 is 22, one whole. It is not 24, which is only a half.

Lesson 5 of 8

Taking away

Subtracting works just like adding. Make the bottoms match, then take away the tops.

The crossed-out pieces in the picture are the ones you take away. What’s left is the answer.

Remember

Make the bottoms the same. Subtract the tops. Keep the bottom. Simplify.

Lesson 6 of 8

Multiplying fractions

This is the easiest one: no common bottom needed. In fractions, “of” means “times”, so 12 × 34 means “half of three quarters”.

In the square, the purple columns are 34. The orange rows take 12 of the square. The green boxes, shaded both ways, are the answer.

Remember

Top × top. Bottom × bottom. Then simplify.

Whole numbers

Write a whole number over 1. 3 × 25 = 31 × 25 = 65.

Lesson 7 of 8

Dividing fractions

34 ÷ 18 asks a simple question: how many eighths fit into three quarters? The picture shows 6.

To work it out without a picture, use Keep, Change, Flip. Keep the first fraction. Change ÷ into ×. Flip the second fraction upside down. Then multiply as in lesson 6.

Remember

Keep. Change. Flip. Then multiply.

Why the answer grows

Dividing by a fraction smaller than 1 gives a bigger answer, because lots of small pieces fit inside.

Lesson 8 of 8

Mixed numbers

When the top is bigger than the bottom, you have more than one whole. 73 is 7 thirds: that’s 2 whole things and 1 third left over. Written as a mixed number, it is 213.

A fraction like 73, with the top bigger than the bottom, is called an improper fraction. Both ways of writing it are correct.

Remember

To a mixed number: divide the top by the bottom. The remainder is the new top.
Back again: whole × bottom + top.

Before you calculate

Turn mixed numbers into improper fractions before you add, subtract, multiply or divide them.
